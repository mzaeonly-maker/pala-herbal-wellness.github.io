Rosemary is an aromatic herb used in cooking and traditional personal-care practices. Its fragrant leaves are sometimes prepared as a rinse for the hair and scalp.

Interest in rosemary has also grown because of research into rosemary oil and hair growth. However, rosemary water and concentrated rosemary essential oil are different preparations, and evidence about one should not automatically be applied to the other.

## Why Is Rosemary Used for Hair Care?

Rosemary contains naturally occurring plant compounds, including rosmarinic acid and other antioxidants. Traditionally, it has been used in personal-care preparations for its fragrance and refreshing feel.

People explore rosemary rinses as part of a routine intended to:

- Refresh the scalp after washing.
- Add a botanical ingredient to hair care.
- Enjoy the herb's natural fragrance.
- Explore traditional herbal practices.

**What does the evidence say?** Research on rosemary oil for hair loss is limited, and it does not establish that homemade rosemary water grows hair or prevents hair loss. More research is needed.

## How to Prepare Rosemary Water

A simple infusion is easy to make at home. This recipe is for external use only.

### Ingredients

- One small sprig of fresh rosemary or about one teaspoon of dried rosemary.
- One cup of clean water.
- A clean pot and strainer.
- A clean container.

### Preparation

1. Wash fresh rosemary thoroughly. If using dried rosemary, choose a clean product intended for food or personal use.
2. Bring one cup of water to a boil.
3. Turn off the heat and add the rosemary.
4. Cover and steep for 10–15 minutes.
5. Allow the liquid to cool completely.
6. Strain carefully so no leaves remain.
7. Use the infusion while fresh.

You do not need to boil rosemary for a long time. Steeping it in hot water is a simple way to prepare a small batch.

## How to Use the Rinse

After shampooing, gently pour a small amount of the cooled rosemary water over your scalp and hair. Keep it away from your eyes.

For your first use, rinse it off after a short period rather than leaving it on for hours. Stop using it if your scalp becomes itchy, red, dry or irritated.

Prepare only what you need for one use. Homemade herbal water has no validated preservative system, so avoid storing leftovers at room temperature or keeping them for repeated use.

## Rosemary Water Is Not Rosemary Essential Oil

This distinction is important.

Rosemary water is a mild infusion made with water and rosemary leaves. Rosemary essential oil is a concentrated product containing volatile compounds.

**Never apply undiluted rosemary essential oil directly to your scalp.** It can cause irritation or allergic reactions. Essential oils require appropriate dilution, and advice for using rosemary water should not be used as a dilution guide for essential oil.

Do not swallow essential oil or add it to drinking water as a home remedy.

## Can Rosemary Make Hair Grow?

Some preliminary research has examined rosemary oil for hair loss, but the evidence remains limited. Results from a particular oil preparation do not prove that rosemary water has the same effect.

Hair thinning can have many causes, including genetics, nutritional problems, hormonal changes, scalp conditions and certain medicines. A rinse cannot be relied upon to diagnose or treat these causes.

If hair loss is sudden, persistent, painful or accompanied by bald patches, consult a qualified healthcare professional.

## Safety Tips

- Patch-test a small amount of cooled rosemary water on your skin and wait 24–48 hours before wider use.
- Avoid applying it to broken, inflamed or infected skin.
- Keep it away from your eyes.
- Stop immediately if you develop a rash, burning or swelling.
- Use correctly identified rosemary from a clean source.
- Keep homemade preparations and essential oils away from children.

People with known plant allergies or very sensitive scalps should be especially cautious.

## Final Thoughts

Rosemary water is a straightforward way to explore a traditional botanical ingredient in your hair-care routine. It is not a proven hair-growth treatment, and it should not replace professional assessment for ongoing hair loss.

Good scalp care begins with gentle washing, avoiding excessive tension and heat, and seeking help when a problem persists.
