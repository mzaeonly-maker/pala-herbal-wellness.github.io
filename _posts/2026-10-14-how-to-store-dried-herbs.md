Why Proper Herb Storage Matters

Dried herbs are convenient because removing moisture helps extend their storage life.

However, dried herbs can still lose their flavour, aroma and quality when they are exposed to moisture, heat, sunlight or air for long periods.

Learning how to store dried herbs properly can help maintain their quality and reduce unnecessary waste.

Whether you keep herbs for cooking, herbal tea or general household use, simple storage practices can make a significant difference.

What Can Damage Dried Herbs?

Several factors can reduce the quality of dried herbs.

The most common include:

- Moisture
- Direct sunlight
- Heat
- Air exposure
- Insects and contamination
- Strong surrounding odours
- Poor-quality containers

Moisture is particularly important because dried plant material can absorb water from the surrounding environment.

Once herbs become damp, the risk of mould and deterioration increases.

Choose the Right Container

One of the simplest ways to protect dried herbs is to use a clean, dry, airtight container.

Suitable options can include:

- Glass jars with secure lids
- Food-safe airtight containers
- Properly sealed herbal storage containers

The container should be completely dry before you put the herbs inside.

Avoid storing dried herbs in containers that previously contained substances with strong smells unless they have been thoroughly cleaned and are suitable for food or herbal storage.

Keep Dried Herbs Away From Moisture

Moisture is one of the biggest enemies of dried herbs.

Keep herbs away from:

- Sinks
- Steam from cooking
- Damp cupboards
- Wet utensils
- Areas with high humidity

For this reason, storing dried herbs immediately above a kettle, cooker or other source of steam may not be ideal.

Always use completely dry hands or utensils when handling stored herbs.

Protect Herbs From Direct Sunlight

Light can gradually affect the colour, aroma and quality of dried plant material.

Store herbs in a dark cupboard or another location away from direct sunlight.

Opaque containers can provide additional protection from light.

If transparent glass jars are used, placing them inside a dark cupboard is preferable to leaving them on a sunny windowsill.

Avoid Excessive Heat

High temperatures can contribute to the loss of some aromatic compounds in herbs.

A cool, dry storage area is generally preferable.

Avoid keeping dried herbs close to:

- Ovens
- Cookers
- Radiators
- Hot appliances
- Areas exposed to prolonged heat

A kitchen cupboard can work well if it remains cool and dry.

Label Your Herbs

Clear labelling makes herbal storage safer and more organised.

A useful label can include:

- Name of the herb
- Date purchased or dried
- Source, if relevant
- Important preparation information

For example:

Peppermint — Purchased October 2026

Labelling is especially helpful when several dried herbs look similar.

How Long Do Dried Herbs Last?

Dried herbs do not remain at peak quality forever.

Over time, they may gradually lose:

- Aroma
- Flavour
- Colour
- Potency of some naturally occurring compounds

The exact storage life varies depending on the herb, how well it was dried, packaging and storage conditions.

Instead of relying only on a fixed number of months or years, check the herb regularly for changes in quality.

Commercial products should be used according to the manufacturer's best-before or storage guidance.

Signs That Dried Herbs Should Be Discarded

Do not use dried herbs if they show obvious signs of deterioration.

Look for:

- Visible mould
- Unusual moisture
- Clumping caused by dampness
- An unusual or unpleasant smell
- Insect contamination
- Significant discolouration
- Other visible signs of spoilage

When in doubt about a questionable product, it is safer to discard it rather than consume it.

Do not try to remove visible mould and use the remaining material.

Can You Store Dried Herbs in Plastic Bags?

Plastic bags can be useful for short-term storage when they are food-safe and properly sealed.

However, a good airtight container can provide better protection against moisture and air exposure.

If using a bag, make sure it is clean, dry and securely sealed.

For longer storage, consider using a suitable airtight container and keeping it in a cool, dark location.

Should Dried Herbs Be Refrigerated?

Most properly dried herbs do not require refrigeration.

A refrigerator can introduce moisture and condensation, particularly if containers are repeatedly opened and closed.

For ordinary dried herbs, a cool, dry and dark cupboard is often a more practical storage location.

Follow the manufacturer's instructions for commercially packaged herbal products.

How to Store Herbal Tea

Dried herbal tea should also be protected from moisture, heat, light and strong odours.

Keep tea bags or loose herbal tea in a sealed container or its original packaging if it provides adequate protection.

This is especially useful for aromatic herbs such as peppermint, chamomile and other strongly scented plants.

How to Store Dried Ginger

Dried ginger should be stored in a clean, airtight container.

Keep it away from moisture and direct sunlight.

If the dried ginger develops mould, unusual moisture or a strange smell, it should not be used.

Fresh ginger is different from dried ginger and requires different storage conditions.

How to Store Dried Hibiscus

Dried hibiscus should also be protected from humidity.

Keep it in a dry, airtight container away from sunlight.

Because hibiscus is often used to prepare tea, make sure the dried material remains clean and dry before preparing it.

How to Store Fresh Herbs

Fresh herbs should not be stored in exactly the same way as dried herbs.

Fresh leaves contain significant amounts of water and can deteriorate more quickly.

Depending on the herb, fresh herbs may be refrigerated or stored using other appropriate methods.

Do not put fresh, damp herbs into an airtight container for long-term storage and assume they will behave like properly dried herbs.

How to Tell If Dried Herbs Have Lost Quality

Some herbs may still look acceptable but have lost much of their aroma or flavour.

If a dried herb has become noticeably weaker in smell, colour or flavour, its quality may have declined.

This does not necessarily mean that every old herb is dangerous, but it may no longer provide the quality expected from a fresh, properly stored product.

When purchasing commercial products, follow the manufacturer's storage and expiry guidance.

Simple Dried Herb Storage Checklist

Before storing dried herbs, check that:

- The herbs are completely dry.
- The container is clean and dry.
- The lid seals properly.
- The storage area is cool.
- The herbs are protected from direct sunlight.
- The container is away from moisture.
- The herbs are clearly labelled.
- The product shows no signs of mould or contamination.

These simple steps can help protect your herbs and reduce unnecessary waste.

Why Good Storage Supports Responsible Herbal Use

Good storage is more than an organisational habit.

Poorly stored herbs can become contaminated or lose their desirable qualities.

Proper storage therefore forms part of responsible herbal use.

It is also important to remember that correct storage cannot make an unsafe or incorrectly identified plant safe.

Always know what plant you are using and obtain herbal products from reliable sources.

Final Thoughts

Knowing how to store dried herbs properly is an important part of using herbal products responsibly.

Keep dried herbs dry, cool and protected from direct sunlight. Use clean airtight containers, label your herbs and regularly check for signs of deterioration.

When an herb develops mould, unusual moisture, insect contamination or an unpleasant smell, discard it.

Good storage helps preserve the quality of dried herbs while making everyday herbal use simpler and more organised.
