Herbs are all around us. They can be found in gardens, kitchens, farms and local markets. Many plants have been used by different communities for generations as food, beverages, aromas and traditional wellness practices.

## Herbs in Everyday Life

You may already use herbs without thinking much about them.

Common examples include:

- Mint
- Ginger
- Lemongrass
- Rosemary
- Basil
- Turmeric
- Aloe vera

These plants can have different culinary, aromatic and traditional uses depending on the plant and the community using it.

## Traditional Uses of Herbs

Across different cultures, people have developed knowledge about plants and their traditional uses.

Some herbs are prepared as teas, while others are used in cooking, natural fragrances or traditional practices.

Traditional use is valuable cultural knowledge, but it does not automatically mean that an herb is safe or effective for every person or condition.

## Learning About an Herb

Before using a herb, take time to learn about it.

Consider finding out:

- What the plant is called
- Which part of the plant is used
- How it is normally prepared
- How it has traditionally been used
- Possible precautions
- The quality and ingredients of commercial products

Clear information can help you make more informed choices.

## Natural Does Not Always Mean Risk-Free

It is important to remember that natural products can still have effects on the body.

Some herbs may interact with medicines or may not be suitable for certain people.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medicines or managing a health condition, speak with a qualified healthcare professional before using herbal products.

## Start With Curiosity

You don't have to become an expert overnight.

Choose one herb that interests you and learn about it. Discover where it comes from, how people traditionally use it and what precautions are associated with it.

The more you learn, the better prepared you are to make informed everyday choices.
